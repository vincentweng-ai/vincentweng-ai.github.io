Fed Holds as Foreign Investors Pull NT$68.8 Billion from Taiwan Stocks

Thursday, October 29, 2026 Data: US close Oct 28 · Taiwan close Oct 29

The Takeaway

Oil up, foreign money out

The Fed kept rates at 3.75%–4.00%, but two officials voted for a hike and futures now price roughly an 85% chance of a December increase. Crude is back above $100. US indexes edged higher on a narrow rally in AI chips. Taiwan's TAIEX jumped 939 points on SK Hynix's results, then gave back 667 points a day later as foreign investors sold a net NT$68.8 billion. Net selling by Taiwan's three major institutional groups has topped NT$1 trillion so far in October. With earnings season in full swing and US midterms next Tuesday, expect a choppier couple of weeks in both markets.

Six Market Signals

TrendChoppy at highs

The S&P 500 is about 1% below its early-October high and the Nasdaq nearly 4%. Taiwan swung up 939 points and down 667 on consecutive days, closing below its 60-day average.

Fund flowsForeign selling

Foreign investors sold a net NT$68.8 billion of Taiwan stocks on Oct 29. Domestic investment trusts bought for a 13th straight day, one of the few steady buyers.

RatesHeadwind

The Fed's hold came with a hawkish tilt. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.04%, its biggest one-day gain since late September.

ConcentrationHigh

The equal-weight S&P 500 slipped while AI chips rallied. In Taiwan, electronics heavyweights led the drop as money rotated into financials and biotech.

GeopoliticsElevated

Tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz hit a wartime high in mid-October, briefly sending Brent to $110. Analysts flag the risk of renewed US strikes on Iran after the midterms.

InflationSticky

US September CPI rose 3.7% year over year, above forecasts. Higher oil makes inflation harder to cool and makes it harder for the Fed to stop hiking.

Electronics heavyweights lead the drop; money rotates into financials and biotech

The TAIEX fell as much as 980 points intraday and briefly broke below 46,000 before closing down 667. MediaTek and Delta Electronics lost about 4%, Quanta about 3%, and Largan about 8%. High-priced names such as ASPEED and King Slide also slipped. First Financial rose about 3%, Chang Hwa Bank, Mega and Hua Nan gained about 1%, and PharmaEssentia also advanced as money moved into defensive sectors.

Memory stocks rode SK Hynix's coattails the day before

Nanya Technology rose 8.16% and Winbond 5.52% on Oct 28 as the TAIEX climbed 939 points. Investors applied SK Hynix's view that memory demand will outrun supply through 2030 to Taiwanese peers. Memory remains one of the most cyclical corners of tech: makers add capacity when prices rise, and oversupply eventually turns the cycle.

▲ Memory

Foreign investors keep pulling money out in October

Reports put October's cumulative net selling by Taiwan's three major institutional groups above NT$1 trillion, with a weaker Taiwan dollar speeding the outflow. Foreign investors are among the largest holders of Taiwan stocks, and their flows move the index heavyweights, which makes them a number worth tracking every day.

The US–Taiwan Link: Cloud Capex Is Taiwan's Order Book

Microsoft spent $45.2 billion and Alphabet $51.4 billion on capex this quarter, mostly on AI servers and data centers. That money flows through foundry, server assembly, cooling and power supply, and Taiwanese companies sit at many of those steps. Capex lines in US earnings reports are among the best real-time gauges of demand for Taiwan's AI supply chain. The past two days showed the link in action: SK Hynix's strong quarter lifted Taiwan, and Alphabet's miss preceded the pullback.
